3. The correction

Using the official pre-submission table for the seasons actually named, the decrease is(1658.8−1039.1)/1658.8≈37.36%, or 37.4%. It is about 5.6 percentagepoints smaller than 43%. CurrentDestatis 42321-0001 also gives approximately 1.659 million versus 1.039 milliontonnes; small provisional revisions do not account for the gap. The table measures domestic sales of nitrogen nutrient, not fertiliser-product mass or directly observed field application.

4. What this does not show

This does not establish the effect of the law on water quality, why fertiliser sales fell, actual nutrient use on farms or the desirability of nutrient balances. Weather, prices, stock changes and other factors can affect sales. A different starting season or clerical year slip is a possible explanation, but the record tests the years actually printed and does not infer the source or intent of the mistake.

Minor editorial judgment: the stated decline is overstated by about 5.6 percentagepoints, but a large decline remains and the passage is introductory background rather than a quantified estimate of compliance costs or a direct causal effect.
