Tenancy law reform II

Statement by Haus & Grund

Changing tenancy rules, including a higher cost threshold for simplified modernisation rent increases.

property owners’ association · found 25 October 2026

They want
Automatically index the proposed EUR 20,000 threshold to construction prices.
They said
On a 2021=100 base, the residential construction-price index was about 107 in 2019 and above 150 in 2023.
The source says
An official pre-submission table gives annual 90.2 and 126.1 on that base. The implied rise is nevertheless still about 40%, so the numerical growth argument changes little.
What it changes
Corrects index-level/base reporting. It does not materially remove the evidence of substantial construction-price increases or the general case for discussing indexation.

3. The correction

Use the correctly labelled2021-base index levels. The official January 2025 historical table reports 2019 annual 90.2 and 2023 annual 126.1. Their independently calculated change is(126.1/90.2-1)*100=39.8004%, close to 40%. Different within-year endpoints may exceed 40%; no separate false-growth finding is made.

4. What this does not show

Ancillary minor issue. A rescaling or clerical mistake is a strong benign explanation, and the implied relative movement remains approximately right. The author does not specify particular months or an annual-average definition, so the precise wording about growth above 40% is not separately adjudicated. Construction prices do not alone decide the appropriate threshold, but this screen does not determine whether doubling it or indexing it is justified. No intent is inferred.

Minor: wrong reported index levels and base, with little practical change to the approximately 40% growth premise. This should not be presented as a major inflation exaggeration or a refutation of the policy request.
