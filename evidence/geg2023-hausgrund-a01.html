Minor error Favours the author’s request

Buildings Energy Act 2023 (GEG)

Statement by Haus & Grund

A 65% renewable-heat requirement for newly installed building heating systems.

property owners’ association · found 24 October 2026

They want
Additional hardship exceptions, including for partially renovated and unrenovated buildings.
They said
Only a heat-pump and gas/oil hybrid is technically feasible in older buildings.
The source says
Contemporary measurements include older buildings heated by heat pumps without a gas/oil boiler.
What it changes
Removes the blanket technical premise that older buildings necessarily need a fossil hybrid, weakening that rationale for broader exceptions.

1. What the submission says

„Für ältere Gebäude ist überdies nur eine noch teurere hybride Lösung mit Wärmepumpe und Gas- bzw. Ölheizung technisch umsetzbar.“

Translation: “For older buildings, moreover, only an even more expensive hybrid solution with a heat pump and gas or oil heating is technically feasible.”

3. The correction

Fraunhofer's July 2020 report documents building ID 363, built in 1973, with a ground-source heat pump and electric backup, without a gas/oil boiler. In July 2018–June 2019 its measured annual performance factor was 3.7; recorded heater-to-compressor electricity ratios were 0%/0% for room heating/hot water. Original walls and windows remained, with roof insulation and a bathroom-radiator change recorded.

4. What this does not show

One counterexample refutes the unqualified exclusivity claim; it does not establish suitability, affordability or sufficient capacity for every older building. This house had earlier improvements, including roof insulation. The later section 2.4 expressly contemplates heat pumps covering full heat demand and avoiding costly hybrids. The finding therefore concerns the opening categorical sentence, rather than the document as a whole. Subsidy and hardship policy remain separate judgments.

Minor after full-context review: the opening summary overstates technical necessity for a broad older-building category, but section 2.4 explicitly recognises heat pumps covering full demand and avoiding hybrids. This narrows the material effect on the overall hardship request. High confidence in the literal mismatch is separate from this conservative severity judgment.
