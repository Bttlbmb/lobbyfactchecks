In short

They want
Support transfer of exposure records to the central exposure database.
They said
Their supporting reference identifies a 2018 Nienhaus infection-prevention paper as IJERPH 15(2), article 206.
The source says
That precise journal location belongs to a Zou et al. review of Baduanjin exercise for pain and sleep, not the cited author or infection-prevention title.
What it changes
The printed citation cannot be used to locate the infection-prevention study that the author invokes for its exposure-record argument. Correcting the bibliography would remove this source-identity error. No claim is made that a different, correctly identified Nienhaus paper could not support the general argument.

Correction in detail

IJERPH 2018;15(2):206 is the paper by Zou, Yeung, Quan, Boyden and Wang, DOI 10.3390/ijerph 15020206, on exercise, musculoskeletal pain and sleep. Its original article metadata and MDPI-deposited Crossref record agree. The Nienhaus author/title and these journal coordinates cannot identify the same article as printed. The intended replacement reference has not been established.

What this does not show

This is a bibliographic identity error, not proof that long-term exposure registers lack value, that the underlying occupational-health propositions are false, or that no such study exists anywhere. No inference about intention or how the error arose. A clerical conflation or incorrectly copied reference remains plausible. No actual author response or specialist review.

Minor. The mismatch frustrates verification of one supporting reference, but no change to a quantitative policy effect or the desirability of exposure records has been demonstrated. It does not justify major severity.
