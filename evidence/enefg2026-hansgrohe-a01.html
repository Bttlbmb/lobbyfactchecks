Minor error Favours the author’s request

Energy Efficiency Act (EnEfG)

Statement by Hansgrohe SE

An ongoing bill revising energy-efficiency obligations, including annual savings by public bodies.

company · submitted 17 April 2026 · found 24 October 2026

They want
Explicitly recognise efficient hot-water fixtures as a public-sector energy-saving contribution.
They said
The EU requires new publicly owned buildings to be zero-emission from 1 January 2027.
The source says
The enacted directive sets 1 January 2028; the submission advances that deadline by one year.
What it changes
Makes the cited regulatory development one year less immediate, weakening a timing reason for adding explicit hot-water-efficiency recognition.

1. What the submission says

Translation: “must meet the standard of zero-emission buildings from 1 January 2027”

„ab dem 1. Januar 2028 neue Gebäude, die sich im Eigentum von öffentlichen Einrichtungen befinden“

Translation: “from 1 January 2028 new buildings owned by public bodies”

3. The correction

Replace the claimed EU deadline with 1 January 2028. Article 7(1)(a) of the original adopted Directive (EU) 2024/1275 applies the zero-emission requirement to new buildings owned by public bodies from that date; other new buildings follow in 2030. This was already the adopted timetable when the 17 April 2026 submission was written.

4. What this does not show

Does not establish that recognising hot-water savings is unnecessary, that efficient fixtures fail to save energy, or that the underlying building-efficiency trend is false. The submission separately invokes existing savings potential and procurement clarity. The directive also permits specified exemptions, and national rules can be earlier or more stringent; the disputed sentence expressly attributes the 2027 date to the EU directive, rather than identifying an earlier German rule. A date slip is a plausible explanation, without any inference of intent.

Minor: one year changes the immediacy of an auxiliary regulatory reason, while the explicit request and independent savings arguments remain. This is not a claim about the size of realised savings.
