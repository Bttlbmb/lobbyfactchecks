3. The correction

The cited 33.5% describes selected nonalcohol drug principal diagnoses in DSHS services, not all addiction-related diagnoses. The original DSHS annual report separately reports cannabis as 9.2% of its broader inpatient principal-diagnosis distribution, based on completed treatments; that is contextual corroboration, not a national inpatient rate or a recalculation from the REITOX totals.

4. What this does not show

The restricted 33.5% figure is valid. DSHS is a selected treatment-service sample, not the national hospital population. The annual report’s 9.2% concerns principal diagnoses among completed treatments in its specified sample and is not a corrected national rate. Total treatment volume and completed-treatment analytic bases must not be mixed. The adjacent hospital-treatment count belongs to another dataset. Cannabis-related harm and treatment demand remain real; no policy conclusion follows automatically. The finding concerns the scope of the cited figure; no national proportion or share including all secondary diagnoses is determined.

Major: removing the alcohol exclusion substantially broadens the represented burden. In the original DSHS report, alcohol accounts for 68.6% and cannabis for 9.2% of the broader principal-diagnosis distribution; the restricted 33.5% cannot be presented as the all-addiction share. This editorial materiality judgment is separate from direction and policy merits.
