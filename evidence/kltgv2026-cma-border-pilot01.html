Minor error Favours the author’s request

Carbon-dioxide pipeline ordinance (KLtgV)

Statement by Carbon Management Allianz

Uniform safety requirements for constructing, operating and inspecting carbon-dioxide pipelines.

carbon management industry association · found 25 October 2026

In short

They want
Adopt the ordinance promptly and accelerate permitting through nationwide procedures.
They said
Transport pipelines cross borders and traverse several German states by definition.
The source says
The relevant statutory definition describes what a CO₂ pipeline transports and its associated equipment; crossing a state border is not a defining condition.
What it changes
Narrows one premise for nationwide uniform permitting and inspection procedures: some projects can fall entirely within one Land. The practical case for common national rules across a connected network remains.

Correction in detail

CO₂ transport pipelines may cross national or Land borders, but crossing borders is not part of the KSpTG definition. Section 3 number 6 defines CO₂ pipelines by their transport function and associated facilities; number 6 a also defines a network without a multiple-Land condition. The draft incorporates that scope. Describe multi-Land routes as an important use case rather than an unavoidable defining feature of every transport pipeline.

What this does not show

The author may be using transport pipelines informally to mean a planned interregional backbone, rather than every pipeline within the ordinance. Such a backbone can cross several Länder. The explicit by-definition wording nevertheless overstates the relevant legal category. No claim is made that national uniformity is unnecessary, that a particular project stays inside one Land, or that CO₂ infrastructure is undesirable. This is a minor supporting-premise correction, not a refutation of the central policy request. No intent is inferred.

Minor: an express categorical definition is inaccurate and supports the argument for uniform national procedures. Correcting it leaves the broader multi-Land-network and investment-certainty arguments intact; no numerical cost or safety conclusion changes.
