Major error Favours the author’s request

Hydrogen acceleration act

Expert statement by Helmut Waniczek

Faster permitting, procurement and legal procedures for hydrogen infrastructure.

hearing 25 September 2024 · found 24 October 2026

In short

They want
Oppose acceleration on the stated ground that technical and economic prerequisites are missing.
They said
No industrial gas turbine anywhere had operated on pure hydrogen.
The source says
An industrial SGT-400 turbine had run on 100% hydrogen in France in 2023.
What it changes
Contradicts the categorical claim of zero prior industrial-turbine operation, weakening the stated technical-prerequisites argument against acceleration.

Their wording

„Bisher wurde weltweit noch keine einzige industrielle Gasturbine mit reinem Wasserstoff betrieben.“

English: “So far, not a single industrial gas turbine worldwide has been operated on pure hydrogen.”

Correction in detail

The 13 October 2023 Siemens Energy/ENGIE/Centrax report describes operation of an industrial SGT-400 turbine at the Smurfit Kappa site in Saillat-sur-Vienne, France, on 100% renewable hydrogen. A separate NEDO primary report records 100%-hydrogen turbine heat-and-power delivery in Kobe in April 2018.

What this does not show

An implicit reading of industrial as routine commercial or market-ready utility operation is possible. The counterexamples were demonstrations or verification tests; they do not establish routine commercial deployment, economics, utility-scale operation or readiness of the substantially larger Leipzig project. The actual sentence gives no commercial-maturity qualification, and the preceding sentence discusses a missing test run. The correction concerns categorical absence of prior operation only. It does not establish affordable hydrogen power or resolve hydrogen supply, safety or scaling. The cited article concerns Leipzig’s SGT-800 model; this finding does not contradict its model-specific account by substituting the different SGT-400.

Why major: the statement asserts complete worldwide absence of a key technical demonstration and uses this in the section arguing that the bill lacks technical prerequisites. Economic and permitting questions remain independent.
