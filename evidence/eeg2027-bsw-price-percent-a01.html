Minor error Works against the author’s request

Renewable Energy Act 2027 (EEG)

Statement by BSW-Solar

Renewable-electricity support and marketing rules, including small rooftop systems.

solar industry association · submitted 1 October 2026 · found 25 October 2026

They want
Preserve the fixed feed-in tariff for rooftop solar.
They said
The cited scenario makes 2025 electricity prices 20 percent higher without solar.
The source says
Its detailed figures imply about 26.3 percent higher without solar;20.8 percent is the reduction in the reverse direction.
What it changes
The printed percentage understates the modelled no-solar price increase; correction strengthens this supporting pro-solar point rather than weakening it.

1. What the submission says

„um durchschnittlich 20 Prozent höher gewesen wäre“

Translation of fragment: “would have been 20 percent higher on average”

2. What the source says

„entspricht einer Reduktion von 20,8 % durch die Erzeugung aus Photovoltaik.“

Translation of fragment: “corresponds to a 20.8 percent reduction due to generation from photovoltaics.”

PDF page 7 · 2025 – Veränderung der Strompreise und ihrer Struktur, lower-left explanation and adjoining bar chart · retrieved 25 October 2026

Open the source ↗

3. The correction

The detailed study chart gives 112.8€/MWh withoutPV and 89.3€/MWh withPV. The reverse comparison is(112.8−89.3)/89.3≈26.32 percent higher withoutPV. The 20.8 percent reduction uses 112.8 as denominator. Preserve the absolute 23.5€/MWh difference.

4. What this does not show

This checks the description of one explicitly cited scenario study, not whether the scenario is the true counterfactual. The study holds other generation capacity fixed, omits grid-related curtailment and some wider economic effects, and says alternative investment responses could reduce the price effect. Neither correction proves that preserving feed-in support is unwise or that a specific household actually realised the modelled saving. No intent is inferred.

Minor: an auxiliary numerical/source-description issue in a much broader submission. The cited scenario still finds substantial solar benefits and the policy conclusion does not depend on this error alone.
