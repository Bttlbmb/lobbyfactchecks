Minor error Favours the author’s request

Annual Tax Act 2026

Statement by DSTG

Annual federal tax changes, including AI development by tax authorities.

tax employees’ union · found 24 October 2026

They want
Retain AI training data for at least three years.
They said
Tax scoring regularly falls within AI Act Annex III 5(d).
The source says
That provision covers emergency calls, dispatch and triage.
What it changes
Removes the cited subsection as support for the classification premise; a properly supported alternative classification could leave the retention argument intact.

3. The correction

Annex III 5(d) concerns emergency-call evaluation, emergency-service dispatch and triage. It does not classify tax scoring. Other provisions may apply to particular tax or enforcement uses; classification requires their actual purpose.

4. What this does not show

Does not classify any actual tax AI, determine that no other provision applies, settle retention necessity, or decide the draft’s lawfulness. A subsection miscitation could explain the mismatch. The author’s conditional argument must be preserved.

Minor after adversarial reassessment: the cited subsection plainly concerns emergency systems, but the established error is its description or citation. Particular tax systems might fall under other provisions, and the following paragraph conditions the remedy argument on high-risk status. The narrower correction does not establish that the broader retention request fails.

What we read around the claim
  • Page 4 says tax-risk scoring regularly counts under the cited annex. Page 5 later qualifies the effective-remedy argument with soweit; that qualification does not change the content of 5(d). Article 9 itself requires lifecycle risk management for systems within scope, but no conclusion on retention duration is made here. A mistaken subsection number is a plausible explanation, but the text also expressly describes tax scoring as regularly covered. No intent is inferred.
