Cannabis Control Act (2018 hearing)

Statement by Bundesärztekammer

Creating a regulated adult cannabis market.

federal medical association · submitted 19 June 2018 · found 24 October 2026

They want
Oppose adult liberalisation on youth-protection grounds.
They said
Colorado traffic deaths caused by marijuana rose 32%.
The source says
The matched statistic counts deaths in crashes involving a marijuana-positive operator; it does not establish causation.
What it changes
Removes a claimed causal quantification from the traffic-safety argument.

3. The correction

The matching 32% and 10%/19.26% figures in RMHIDTA concern marijuana-related traffic fatalities involving operators with positive tests, including combinations with alcohol or other drugs. Positive toxicology does not identify marijuana-caused deaths.

4. What this does not show

This finding concerns the description of the strongly matched statistic, conditional on that source identification. The actual change in deaths causally attributable to cannabis is not established here and could coincidentally have been 32%; the toxicology series does not decide that question. Cannabis can impair driving, and legalisation may have traffic effects. The preceding CaPRis risk association remains a separate argument and is not disproved.

Major: the wording turns a toxicology association into an attributed cause of death, materially strengthening the evidence presented for the road-safety objection.
