Minor error Favours the author’s request

Financial-centre investment act

Statement by Startup-Verband

Improving financing conditions and reducing financial-market bureaucracy, with a companion motion on exit taxation.

startup association · submitted 27 November 2025 · found 25 October 2026

In short

They want
Defer exit tax until an actual sale or exempt shareholders of young companies.
They said
Exit tax is approximately 28% of the fair value of the held stake.
The source says
The tax is calculated on the deemed disposal gain after acquisition costs, rather than automatically on the whole stake value.
What it changes
For holders with a material acquisition-cost basis, describing the tax as a share of total value overstates the amount. Correcting it weakens that numerical burden rationale for the requested deferral or exemption. A dry-income liability can remain on an unrealised gain.

Correction in detail

Section 6(1) AStG deems a disposal at fair value and refers to section 17 EStG. Section 17(2) defines the gain by deducting acquisition costs; the whole fair value is not automatically the tax base. The stated roughly 28% can be close to a maximum-rate illustration where the cost basis is negligible, but the submission does not state that assumption. This finding concerns the base only, not the exact personal rate or all eligibility conditions.

What this does not show

For successful founders whose cost basis is very small relative to current value, the approximation can be close. This review establishes no actual individual overpayment, no typical cost-basis distribution and no size of the effect on startup formation or migration. It does not show that deferral or an exemption is undesirable. No intent is inferred.

Minor: the base is inaccurately described, but its practical numerical effect depends on an unstated cost basis and may be small for the founder cohort. The genuine possibility of tax before cash proceeds remains. No unsupported major label is assigned.
