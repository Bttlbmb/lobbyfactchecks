Minor error Favours the author’s request

Digital violence act

Statement by HateAid

Civil and criminal protection against digital violence, including disclosure of account data.

civil-society organisation · submitted 22 May 2026 · found 24 October 2026

In short

They want
Extend disclosure to telecom and email providers to identify authors.
They said
Even prepaid SIM purchase within the EU requires data verification.
The source says
EU countries have different rules; Finnish anonymous prepaid services supply a counterexample.
What it changes
Narrows the assumed coverage of verified telephone identities supporting the requested extension.

Their wording

„Innerhalb der EU erfordert nämlich selbst die Anschaffung einer Prepaid-Karte die Verifikation von Daten“

English: “Within the EU, even purchasing a prepaid card requires verification of data”

The source’s wording

Polish Council Presidency note, 28 January 2025: Use of prepaid cards to commit terrorist and criminal offenses

“The issue of access to prepaid SIM cards is not regulated at EU level.”

PDF page 2 · Introduction · second, first sentence · retrieved 24 October 2026

Open the source ↗

Correction in detail

There is no general EU requirement. The Presidency note distinguishes Member States that require identification from those allowing anonymous purchases. Finland’s regulator recognises anonymous prepaid service; a Finnish operator’s terms expressly permit service activation without authentication.

What this does not show

The preceding sentence says telephone numbers often permit identification, which can remain true. The paragraph may generalise from German practice. This does not show German identification requirements are absent, disclosure is never useful, or the proposed extension is unwise.

Minor: the geographical generalisation overstates coverage, while domestic and many other numbers can still be identified. The broader policy case survives.

What we read around the claim
  • Read all of section 2.3.a and adjacent sections 2.2 and 2.3.b. The disputed sentence is explicitly EU-wide; the paragraph does not restrict it to German TKG requirements.
