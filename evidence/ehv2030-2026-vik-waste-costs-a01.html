They want
Exempt hazardous-waste incineration from the national carbon market or suspend that market until ETS 2 operates reliably.
They said
Waste-incineration carbon costs arise exclusively in Germany.
The source says
Danish official guidance expressly imposes a CO₂ tax on relevant hazardous-waste incineration from 2015. That qualifies the Germany-only carbon-cost premise, while scope and net burdens still differ.
What it changes
Qualifies the premise of a uniquely German carbon-cost burden supporting removal or suspension of national pricing. It does not eliminate possible differences in coverage, rates, exemptions, compensation or competitive disadvantage.

3. The correction

Distinguish the absence of uniform EU-wide carbon pricing from the existence of national carbon costs elsewhere. The explicitly versioned Danish Tax Agency 2026-1 guidance states that relevant hazardous-waste incineration has been subject to CO₂ tax since 1 January 2015, while separately explaining its ETS exclusion. Its calculation chapter covers non-biodegradable waste used for heat/electricity, with material and use-based exemptions. Thus carbon charges on relevant hazardous incineration were not exclusive to Germany. Earlier Danish/Swedish waste-to-energy ETS evidence is corroborating background, not the sole matched-cohort counterexample.

4. What this does not show

The correction establishes foreign carbon taxation of relevant hazardous-waste incineration, not that every dedicated destruction plant is charged. Danish rules are limited by energy/heat use, non-biodegradable content and exemptions; a pure-destruction or exempt-material case may differ. No equal net cost, no quantified foreign tax payment and no absence of a German competitive burden is asserted. Dedicated hazardous sites can be outside ETS while within a separate carbon tax. Possible state compensation and the incidence of heat-related charges remain distinct from the existence of the tax. No policy-merit or intent conclusion follows.

Minor: a supporting international-uniqueness premise is overstated. The correction does not quantify relative costs or settle the broader competitive burden, and independent implementation objections remain.
