Major error Favours the author’s request

Energy Efficiency Act (EnEfG)

Statement by VIK

A pending revision of German energy-efficiency duties and implementation of the EU Energy Efficiency Directive.

industrial energy users’ association · found 24 October 2026

They want
Delete or weaken the national energy-efficiency targets in § 4 EnEfG.
They said
Germany used about 3,273 TWh of final energy in 2020; reducing it 11.7% gives an EU-consistent 2030 level around 2,890 TWh, making the German 1,867 TWh target excessively strict.
The source says
The 2020 quantity is primary energy, not final energy. The 11.7% EU target also uses modelled 2030 consumption from the 2020 reference scenario, not observed pandemic-year consumption. The derived comparison is invalid.
What it changes
Removes the numerical basis for claiming a 2,890 TWh EU comparator against the 1,867 TWh German target. The primary/final mislabelling inflates the starting final-energy quantity by roughly 40%, while the reference-year substitution independently prevents the calculation from establishing the asserted EU comparator.

1. What the submission says

„Der Deutsche Endenergieverbrauch lag 2020 bei 402 Mio. t. SKE, bzw. rund 3272.682 TWh.“

Translation: “German final energy consumption in 2020 was 402 million tonnes of coal equivalent, or about 3,272.682 TWh.”

3. The correction

The approximately 402 million-tonne figure matches the statistics producer's 2020 primary-energy total, whose 2021 report gives 402.1 million tonnes/11,784 PJ. Its final-energy infographic instead gives 8,341 PJ, about 2,317 TWh. Category definitions and subsequent revisions may change the precise final figure, not convert the primary total into final energy. Separately, EED Article 4 and recital 28 apply 11.7% to modelled 2030 use in the 2020 reference scenario at EU level. National indicative contributions must account for the directive's criteria; multiplying observed German 2020 use by 0.883 does not produce Germany's EU minimum.

4. What this does not show

This does not prove the German target is feasible, economically optimal or legally mandatory at exactly 1,867 TWh. It does not establish motive. The statement correctly mentions the 2020 reference scenario on page 2; the error arises when the next page substitutes a pandemic-year consumption figure. Simply replacing the starting quantity with actual 2020 final energy and multiplying 0.883 would still not calculate the correct EU-based national contribution. The EED contains a binding collective final-energy target, an indicative primary-energy target and national indicative contributions; do not portray the 11.7% as an identical binding reduction imposed on every state.

Major: the mixed-category, mixed-baseline calculation supplies a much looser numerical comparator for the explicit request to remove or weaken § 4. The magnitude and direct argument linkage, rather than disagreement with the requested policy, determine severity.
