Major error Favours the author’s request

Early-start pension

Statement by Deutsches Aktieninstitut

A public pension starting balance for children, with optional privately financed top-ups.

capital-market association · found 25 October 2026

In short

They want
Additional tax support for private top-ups, drawing on foreign junior-investment schemes.
They said
Private contributions from net income are taxed again at payout, making those contributions doubly taxed.
The source says
The addressed draft distinguishes unfunded private contributions from the state-funded balance. Its memorandum expressly retains taxation of the income component for unfunded contributions, rather than taxing the contributed principal again.
What it changes
Removing a supposed second tax on private principal weakens this specific rationale for additional contribution tax support. A policy case for deductions, higher grants or tax-free investment returns may still exist.

Correction in detail

The July consultation draft does not put all private top-ups in the fully taxed state-funded category. Proposed section 29(2) distinguishes the state-funded balance from private contributions and permits private tax support only under the existing conditions of section 10 a or Chapter XI EStG. Article 2 extends section 22 number 5 sentence 1 to the state-funded Frühstartrente; it retains sentence 2 for payments based on unfunded contributions. The memorandum explicitly requires allocation where funded and unfunded contributions coexist. Depending on the payout form, sentence 2 taxes the annuity income share or the difference between the payment and the contributions, with further cross-references. It therefore does not automatically tax the unfunded contributed principal again.

What this does not show

No future individual tax liability, tax rate or effective return is calculated. Parents genuinely cannot deduct their contributions under this draft, and subsequent investment income can still be taxed. State grants and the returns attributable to them are a different, funded category. The finding does not establish that the proposed tax support is undesirable or that foreign schemes are comparable in all respects. It infers no intent.

Major, provisionally: full second taxation of contributed principal would be substantially more restrictive than taxation confined to investment income, and this is the central factual premise in the dedicated tax-support subsection. The record concerns that premise, not all four policy requests in the submission; independent review may reassess severity.
