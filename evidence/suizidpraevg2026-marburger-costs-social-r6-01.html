What it changes
The stated annual amount makes the office appear to have fewer estimated resources and modestly strengthens the demand for more funding. Correcting it does not answer whether the higher estimate is sufficient for all proposed tasks or direct services.

3. The correction

The addressed draft of 25 June 2026 estimates EUR 1.482 million in recurring annual office costs and EUR 1.2 million once for setup. Tables on physical pages 19 and 26 and explanations on pages 20–21 and 27–28 agree. Both costs begin incrementally in 2027; the recurring estimate applies fully from 2029, and setup costs are fully incurred by then. The stated annual amount is EUR 282,000 below the full recurring estimate, about 19.0% of that estimate. No specific underestimation is computed for the phase-in years 2027 or 2028. This corrects the description of estimated costs, rather than an enacted appropriation or actual expenditure.

4. What this does not show

The estimated costs are not legally guaranteed funding or proof of actual spending. The author does not expressly say funding ends after 2029; its period may focus on implementation. No separate termination-date allegation is made. Early costs are phased in, and no amount for 2027 or 2028 is invented. The correction does not establish adequate resources, clinical benefit or the merits of the policy request. No intent is inferred.

Minor: the full annual estimate is understated by about 19%, but the qualitative adequacy argument survives. This is a bounded description of the draft, not proof that more funding is unnecessary.
