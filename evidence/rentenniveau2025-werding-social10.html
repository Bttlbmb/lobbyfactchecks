Pension level and childrearing credits

Expert statement by Martin Werding

Keeping the pension level at 48 percent and extending childrearing credits for births before 1992.

submitted 10 November 2025 · found 25 October 2026

In short

They want
Omit the proposed further increase in childrearing credits.
They said
The 1992 birth-date cutoff was chosen in a reform following the constitutional judgment of 7 July 1992.
The source says
The cutoff already operated from January 1992 under the 1989 reform. This is a narrow chronology issue; later constitutional decisions also supported the cutoff.
What it changes
Corrects the order of judgment and reform in the historical explanation. It does not refute the separate claim that a cutoff can be legally permissible: the 1996 decision expressly found no unconstitutional inequality in this distinction. Correction in detail

The rule crediting three childrearing years for births after 31 December 1991 predates the 7 July 1992 judgment: the Rentenreformgesetz 1992 was enacted 18 December 1989 and took effect 1 January 1992. The 1992 judgment itself discusses that existing expansion in A.I.4(a), and the 1996 decision explicitly says the court already knew it. Describe the expansion and birth-date cutoff as existing at the time of the 1992 judgment, rather than chosen in a subsequent reform.

What this does not show

No finding that the 2025 bill is unconstitutional or constitutionally required, or that equality, fertility incentives or pension costs favour either position. Later reforms did improve the valuation and additive treatment of childrearing periods; the issue concerns the creation of the 1992 birth-date cutoff specifically. No intent is inferred.

Minor: an incorrect chronological description of the origin of a cutoff in a supporting historical paragraph, with the core legal-permissibility argument independently supported by later court reasoning.
