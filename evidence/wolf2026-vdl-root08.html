Minor error Favours the author’s request

Wolf management, Federal Hunting Act

Statement at the committee hearing, 23 Feb 2026

Adding the wolf to federal hunting law and providing management and removal rules.

speaker in the hearing transcript · found 25 October 2026

In short

They want
Fast, straightforward removal of problem wolves and nationwide management rules.
They said
Sheep and goat keepers are affected by 91% of all attacks.
The source says
The matching official 2024 statistic assigns 91% of harmed animals to sheep/goats; the same report assigns 79% of attack events to them.
What it changes
Reduces the matched 2024 attack-event share by approximately 12 percentage points, weakening the stated concentration of attack events on the represented livestock sector. Sheep and goats nevertheless remain by far the largest affected category under both measures.

Correction in detail

Distinguish the share of harmed animals from the share of attack events. The primary report’s 2024 figures 2 and 3 give respectively 91% of 4300 harmed animals and 79% of 1109 attacks for sheep/goats. One attack can harm multiple animals. The oral statement does not name a year or local source; identification of the 91% as this statistic is a strong matching inference, not an explicit citation.

What this does not show

An oral shorthand use of attacks to mean harms is plausible. The 91% animal share is valid and still supports a serious burden. The record does not determine the actual 2025 or 2026 event share, the effectiveness of herd protection, an individual keeper’s loss or whether the proposed removals are appropriate. The report includes killed, injured and missing animals and differing state attribution criteria; it is not simply a confirmed-kills census. No intent is inferred.

Minor: the event-share description overstates the matching figure, but correcting it leaves a clear majority of attacks on sheep/goats and preserves the central claim of disproportionate burden. The numerical difference alone does not make the overall policy argument fail.
