Major error Favours the author’s request

Basic income support reform

Expert statement by van Suntum

Replacing Bürgergeld with a revised means-tested basic-security system, including stronger obligations and benefit reductions.

hearing 23 February 2026 · found 25 October 2026

They want
Go beyond the government bill and introduce compulsory work, including placements in private businesses.
They said
A current Bertelsmann survey shows that the majority of Bürgergeld recipients are not interested in being placed in work.
The source says
The matched survey measures recent job-search behaviour in a restricted sample; not searching in four weeks is not a finding of disinterest. Most non-searchers reported health obstacles.
What it changes
Removes the survey-based majority-disinterest premise used to support stronger compulsory-work obligations. The evidence identifies heterogeneous search barriers and cannot alone establish that compulsion addresses recipients’ willingness.

3. The correction

Describe the result as a majority not actively searching in the previous four weeks in the sampled target group. The survey sampled 25–50-year-old long-term recipients who were unemployed or seeking work, excluding single parents and people in socially insured employment. Reportp 14 finds 54.6% had not searched; p 18 reports health reasons among 74% of non-searchers. This result does not measure the share with no interest in taking a job or accepting placement.

4. What this does not show

The actual proportion interested in work across all recipients is not established here. Recent non-search can sometimes coexist with disinterest. The author may use uninterested as loose shorthand for observed non-search rather than a separately measured attitude, but explicitly attributes it to the survey. The report recognises incentive problems and ambiguous motivation; its findings do not rule out compulsory-work policies or show that every non-searcher is unable or willing to work. The adjacent quarter citing financial incentives is not separately alleged erroneous: the primary report itself uses similar summary shorthand.

Major editorial judgment: converts a behavioural majority into an adverse motivational majority in a central explanation of why ordinary placement will fail and compulsory work is needed. The broader policy proposal also relies on international comparisons and incentives, which are not disproved.

What we read around the claim
  • Read all five author pages(official bundle 167–171), including the preceding reference to labour-market placement and the following quarter-incentives sentence. No definition equating interest with four-week search or survey-population qualification appears.
  • Read primary survey methods(pp 7,11–12), operational definition(pp 13–14), search-time measure(p 17), non-search reasons(p 18), qualitative interpretations(pp 19–25) and conclusions(pp 34–36). The report itself acknowledges ambiguous motives, possible rationalisations and incentive problems; do not flatten this into a finding that motivation never matters.
  • The p 14 weighted search-incidence figure and p 17 search-hours figures use different questions/sample counts; the publisher’s press-release 57% is not substituted for the report’s 54.6%. This is not treated as an additional author error.
  • Survey findings are empirical and do not depend on subsequent amendments; exact bills and motion are archived so the target proposal is not confused with the enacted law.
  • Primary report p 13 explicitly excludes jobcentre-proposed jobs from its operational definition of independent job search. Thus the preceding placement antecedent does not turn the measured independent-search result into a direct measure of receptiveness to placement.
