Abortion access motion

Statement by 1000plus / Profemina

A parliamentary motion requesting measures to improve access to abortion services, including staffing, training and telemedical support.

submitted 14 April 2026 · found 25 October 2026

They want
Reject the motion and prioritise counselling and practical support for continuing pregnancy.
They said
An abortion-rate rise from 56 to 63 per 10,000 women equals 14.3%.
The source says
Using the stated rates, the increase is 12.5%. The percentage exaggerates the rise, but its upward direction remains.
What it changes
Slightly weakens the magnitude used in the argument that rising abortions show no need to expand provision. It does not establish whether access is adequate; national totals alone do not settle regional barriers.

Arithmetic of the displayed rates, not a new causal estimate or assertion that rounded rates are exact underlying rates.

3. The correction

The stated seven-unit rise is 12.5% of the 2015 base 56, not 14.3%. The comparison remains an increase. Official Destatis tables confirm the displayed rates; the RKI historical table reports 2015 as 56.0 to one decimal, which also limits a benign rounding explanation.

4. What this does not show

The absolute counts and upward direction are not refuted. No calculation is shown; a clerical or denominator slip is possible. Published rates are rounded and census-based population series can change. This record corrects the arithmetic of the displayed 56-to 63 comparison rather than deriving an exact harmonised 2015-2024 trend. Historical RKI 2015 rate 56.0 means a hidden rate near 55.5 cannot rescue 14.3% while retaining a 2024 rate rounded to 63. No clinical-risk conclusion or optimal-policy conclusion follows.

Minor: growth is overstated by 1.8 percentage points, while the same national upward trend remains. The opposition also invokes legal, moral, clinical and counselling arguments, which this arithmetic check does not decide.
