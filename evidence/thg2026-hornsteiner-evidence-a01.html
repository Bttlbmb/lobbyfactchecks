Major error Favours the author’s request

Greenhouse-gas quota act

Expert statement by Matthias Hornsteiner

Increasing transport-fuel greenhouse-gas reduction quotas and implementing RED III.

submitted 2 March 2026 · found 25 October 2026

They want
Do not base policy on the climate premises used to justify this bill.
They said
No indications of anthropogenic climate warming are known.
The source says
Published field measurements provide empirical evidence of increasing greenhouse forcing linked to human emissions.
What it changes
Undercuts the categorical absence-of-evidence premise supporting refusal to base policy on anthropogenic-warming concerns.

3. The correction

Known evidence exists. Feldman et al. report statistically significant clear-sky CO 2 surface-forcing increases of 0.2 W/m² per decade at Oklahoma/Alaska sites during 2000–2010, associated with 22 ppm more CO 2. Their analysis combines measured spectra, ancillary observations and radiative-transfer calculations; fossil emissions and fires substantially contributed to the CO 2 increase. This is empirical support for the warming mechanism, not a standalone estimate of global temperature attribution.

4. What this does not show

The measurement is surface radiative forcing at two clear-sky sites, not direct measurement of global temperature sensitivity or the benefit of this bill. It uses model-assisted isolation of CO 2, with reported uncertainties ±0.06/±0.07 W/m² per decade; methods discuss cloud-selection effects. The author may mean no evidence he regards as convincing: later pages reject mainstream research and funding arrangements. That evaluative reading should be distinguished from the literal claim that no indications are known. No intent is inferred.

Major editorial judgment: the claimed absence of any indications is a foundational premise of the submission’s policy objection, rather than an incidental figure. The correction does not establish that any particular fuel quota is efficient or desirable.
