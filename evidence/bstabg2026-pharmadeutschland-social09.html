Health insurance contribution stabilisation

Statement by Pharma Deutschland

Limiting health-insurance spending, including volume-related reductions in reimbursement for digital health applications (DiGA).

industry association · submitted 20 April 2026 · found 25 October 2026

In short

They want
Reject additional price controls for DiGA.
They said
DiGA enter care only after a positive healthcare effect has been demonstrated.
The source says
Lower-risk DiGA may be admitted and reimbursed provisionally while the required effect is still being tested. Permanent admission requires the evidence.
What it changes
Qualifies the premise that all reimbursed DiGA already have a demonstrated positive effect. It weakens that part of the argument that further reimbursement controls would penalise established beneficial care, but does not determine whether the proposed volume reductions are appropriate.

Correction in detail

Distinguish permanent admission from provisional admission for testing. Section 139 e(4) permits lower-risk DiGA to enter the reimbursable directory when the positive-effect evidence is not yet available, subject to other requirements, a plausible rationale and an independent evaluation plan. Permanent admission requires the positive-effect evidence. The general statement that care starts only after demonstration omits an affirmative statutory route to earlier reimbursed care.

What this does not show

Provisional admission is regulated and requires safety, quality, data-protection and evaluation conditions; it is not unrestricted marketing. Missing final evidence is not proof that a particular DiGA is ineffective, unsafe or fails to help patients. No clinical outcome, actual effect size, manufacturer cost or optimal reimbursement price is established. No intent is inferred.

Minor: the statement overgeneralises a relevant evidential safeguard, but established permanently admitted DiGA do have to demonstrate positive effects and the author advances multiple independent objections to price cuts. No false clinical efficacy conclusion is inferred.
